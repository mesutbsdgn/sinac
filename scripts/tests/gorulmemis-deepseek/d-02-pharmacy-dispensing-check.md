# Dispensing Check: Paracetamol and Unit Conversion

## Label reading

- The carton states **1,000 mg** per tablet; that comma is a thousands separator, not a decimal point.
- A trailing zero after a decimal point, such as 5.0 mg written by hand, is a prescribing error and must be queried.

## Maximum daily dose

| Patient | Single dose | Interval | Daily total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult, 70 kg | 1,000 mg | every 6 h | 4,000 mg |

## Unit conversion drill

Serum creatinine 1,2 mg/dL equals 106 micromol/L (1,2 × 88,4 ≈ 106).

## Stock solution

A 10 mL ampoule contains 500 mg. To draw 250 mg, withdraw 5 mL.

## Counselling points

1. Ask about other paracetamol-containing products.
2. Record the weight for patients under 50 kg.
3. Flag any handwritten order with a trailing zero.

## Self-check

1. How many milligrams are in 4 mL of the 500 mg/10 mL ampoule?
2. Is "1,000 mg" a decimal error in English labelling?
