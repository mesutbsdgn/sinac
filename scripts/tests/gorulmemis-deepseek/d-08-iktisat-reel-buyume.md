# Nominal vs Real Growth in One Page

## Setup

- Nominal GDP last year: 1,000 billion.
- Nominal GDP this year: 1,060 billion.
- GDP deflator last year: 100.
- GDP deflator this year: 103.

## Nominal growth

- 1,060 / 1,000 = 1.06, so nominal growth is 6%.

## Real growth

- Real GDP this year = 1,060 / 1.03 = 1,029.1 billion.
- Real growth = 1,029.1 / 1,000 − 1 = 2.9%.
- Shortcut: (1.06 / 1.03) − 1 ≈ 2.9%.

## Reading the result

- Nominal growth overstates output when prices rise.
- A deflator above 100 means prices are higher than in the base year.
- Per-capita figures need population growth subtracted in the same way.

## Practice points

- Keep units consistent: billions here, not millions.
- Round only at the final step.
- Report the base year whenever you quote a deflator.

## Self-check

1. If the deflator were 106, what would real growth be?
2. Why is nominal growth 6% but real growth 2.9%?
3. Which number would you quote in a policy brief?
