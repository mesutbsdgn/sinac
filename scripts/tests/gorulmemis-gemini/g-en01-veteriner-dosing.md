# Canine and Feline Analgesia: Veterinary Dosing Notes

## 1. Species-Specific Safety Principles
Veterinary pharmacology requires strict attention to species differences. Drugs well tolerated in dogs may cause severe toxicity in cats due to limited hepatic glucuronidation. Always verify the target species, route, and withdrawal period for food-producing livestock before administering any medication.

## 2. Weight-Based Analgesic Calculations
When calculating doses in dogs and cats, express the regimen clearly in `mg/kg/dose` or `mg/kg/day` along with the dosing interval.

### Worked Example 1 — Canine Postoperative Pain
- **Species:** Dog, 10 kg body weight
- **Regimen:** Tramadol 5 mg/kg/dose orally every 8 hours
- **Calculation:** For a 10 kg dog at 5 mg/kg/dose, give 80 mg per dose orally.

### Worked Example 2 — Feline Rescue Analgesia
In acute feline pain management, administer .5 mg intravenously as an initial bolus, followed by a maintenance dose of 2.0 mg every 6 hours if pain scores remain elevated.

## 3. Monitoring and Toxicity Alerts
- Never administer acetaminophen (paracetamol) to cats; doses as low as 10 mg/kg can induce methemoglobinemia (Merck Veterinary Manual).
- Reassess pain scores every 4 hours using a validated feline or canine scale.

## Self-Check
1. Why are cats particularly vulnerable to acetaminophen toxicity?
2. What is the correct single dose for a 12 kg dog prescribed 4 mg/kg/dose?

### Answers
1. Cats have a deficiency in UDP-glucuronosyltransferase enzymes required for hepatic glucuronidation.
2. 12 kg × 4 mg/kg/dose = 48 mg.
