# Clinical Nutrition: Energy Requirements and Macronutrients

## 1. Energy Units and Basal Metabolic Rate
In nutrition and dietetics, energy intake is expressed in kilocalories (kcal) or kilojoules (kJ), where 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ. Resting energy expenditure can be estimated using validated predictive equations such as Mifflin-St Jeor and adjusted for physical activity level.

## 2. Macronutrient Energy Yields and Ranges
According to EFSA Dietary Reference Values (2017):
- **Carbohydrates:** 4 kcal/g; recommended intake is 45–60% of total daily energy.
- **Proteins:** 4 kcal/g; reference intake for healthy adults is 0.83 g/kg body weight per day.
- **Fats:** 9 kcal/g; recommended range is 20–35% of total daily energy, limiting saturated fatty acids.

## 3. Worked Energy Calculation
Consider an adult requiring 2000 kcal per day with a macronutrient target of 50% carbohydrate, 20% protein, and 30% fat:
- Carbohydrate: 1000 kcal ÷ 4 kcal/g = 250 g
- Protein: 400 kcal ÷ 4 kcal/g = 100 g
- Fat: 600 kcal ÷ 9 kcal/g ≈ 67 g

## 4. Micronutrients and Portion Literacy
Adequate vitamin and mineral intake should be achieved primarily through diverse whole foods. Portion guidance for patients should pair metric weights (grams) with household measures.

## Self-Check
1. How many kilojoules correspond to 500 kcal?
2. How many grams of protein supply 320 kcal?

### Answers
1. 500 × 4.184 = 2092 kJ.
2. 320 ÷ 4 = 80 g.
