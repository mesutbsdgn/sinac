# Plate Tectonics, Stratigraphy, and Fault Zones

## 1. Lithospheric Plates and Boundary Types
Earth's outer shell is divided into rigid lithospheric plates that move over the ductile asthenosphere at rates of several centimeters per year. Three primary boundary interactions shape global geology:
- **Divergent boundaries:** Plates move apart, generating new oceanic crust at mid-ocean ridges.
- **Convergent boundaries:** Plates collide or subduct, creating volcanic arcs and mountain belts.
- **Transform boundaries:** Plates slide horizontally past one another along strike-slip fault zones, producing shallow earthquakes.

## 2. Rock Cycle and Stratigraphic Principles
Sedimentary, igneous, and metamorphic rocks continuously transform through weathering, burial, melting, and uplift. In undeformed sedimentary sequences, the principle of superposition states that younger strata overlie older strata. Mineral assemblages and fossil markers allow geologists to correlate layers across regions.

## 3. Geological Time Reference
Numerical ages in stratigraphy are expressed in mega-annums (Ma, millions of years ago) following the International Commission on Stratigraphy chart (ICS v2026/06). For example, the Cretaceous–Paleogene boundary is dated at 66 Ma.

## 4. Reading Geological Cross-Sections
Every geological map or cross-section should include a scale bar, north arrow, legend of lithological units, and data source (e.g., USGS or national geological survey).
