# Baroque Painting and Chiaroscuro

## 1. Historical Context and Visual Style
Emerging in Rome around 1600, the Baroque style emphasized drama, emotional immediacy, and theatrical lighting. Unlike the balanced symmetry of High Renaissance painting, Baroque artists favored diagonal compositions that draw the viewer directly into the scene.

## 2. Caravaggio and Tenebrism
Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio (born 29 September 1571, Milan; died 1610) pioneered dramatic chiaroscuro—sharp contrasts between deep shadow and intense directional light.
- **Key work:** *The Calling of Saint Matthew* (1599–1600), oil on canvas, 322 × 340 cm, Contarelli Chapel, San Luigi dei Francesi, Rome.
- **Visual analysis:** A diagonal beam of light enters from an unseen upper-right window, guiding the viewer's eye toward the gesture of Christ.

## 3. Rembrandt and Psychological Realism
In the Dutch Republic, Rembrandt van Rijn (born 15 July 1606, Leiden; died 1669) adapted chiaroscuro to convey psychological depth rather than theatrical spectacle.
- **Key work:** *The Night Watch* (1642), oil on canvas, 363 × 437 cm, Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam.
