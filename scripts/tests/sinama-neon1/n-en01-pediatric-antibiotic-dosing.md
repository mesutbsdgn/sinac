# Pediatric Clerkship Guide: Empiric Antibiotic Dosing

Pediatric antimicrobial orders always start with a verified kilogram weight and a clear daily division schedule (q6h, q8h, or q12h). Always double-check whether the drug reference lists a **per-day** or **per-dose** figure before entering an inpatient order.

## 1. Worked Inpatient Dosing Examples

- Consider a 24 kg child treated with ampicillin 200 mg/kg/day divided q6h. The total daily dose equals 4,800 mg, so each q6h administration is 1,200 mg IV.
- For an 18 kg child prescribed clindamycin at 40 mg/kg/day divided q6h, each individual dose is **240 mg** IV.
- Adolescents weighing 50 kg receive amoxicillin 1,000 mg orally every 8 hours for severe beta-lactamase-negative infections.
- Acetaminophen dosing is 15 mg/kg/dose q6h, not to exceed a maximum of 4 g/day in adolescents and adults.

## 3. Order Safety and Stewardship

- Safety reminder: Never write .5 mg or 5.0 mg on pediatric orders; always document 0.5 mg and 5 mg.
- Verify renal function before continuing vancomycin or aminoglycosides beyond 48 hours.
- Narrow empiric broad-spectrum therapy as soon as blood or CSF culture susceptibilities return.
- Transition from IV to oral antibiotics once the child is afebrile for 24 hours, hemodynamically stable, and tolerating oral fluids.
