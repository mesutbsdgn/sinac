# Critical Care Nursing: Heparin Infusion Titration

Unfractionated heparin (UFH) remains a cornerstone anticoagulant in intensive care because its short half-life allows rapid titration and reversal with protamine sulfate. Every bag change and rate adjustment requires independent verification by two licensed nurses.

## 1. Standard VTE Weight-Based Protocol

The institutional ICU premixed bag contains 25,000 units of heparin in 250 mL of D5W, yielding a concentration of 100 units/mL.

- Case study: Patient R. (64-year-old male, 75 kg) was admitted with acute pulmonary embolism.
- For a 75 kg patient on the VTE protocol, the 80 units/kg IV bolus is 6,000 units, followed by 18 units/kg/hr at 1,350 units/hr (13.5 mL/hr using a 100 units/mL bag).
- Joint Commission Do-Not-Use alert: Never write trailing zeros such as 5.0 units or naked decimals such as .5 mg; write 5 units and 0.5 mg instead.

## 3. Hemodynamic and Coagulation Monitoring

- Obtain baseline aPTT, hemoglobin, hematocrit, and platelet count prior to the loading bolus.
- Verify arterial blood gas pH 7.40 and aPTT ratio 2.0 before adjusting the pump; keep the transducer aligned 5.0 cm below the sternal angle.
- Draw follow-up aPTT specimens every 6 hours from a limb contralateral to the heparin infusion to avoid sample contamination.
- Monitor daily platelets for heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), especially between days 5 and 10 of therapy.

## 4. Shift Handover Checklist

1. Trace the heparin tubing physically from the smart-pump channel to the dedicated vascular access port.
2. Confirm smart-pump guardrails match the active weight-based VTE library entry.
3. Document recent bleeding assessments, including neurologic checks and stool guaiac if indicated.
