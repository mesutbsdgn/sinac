# Emergency Medicine Conference: Acute Ischemic Stroke Triage

Time lost during stroke activation translates directly into irreversible neuronal injury. Emergency teams target a door-to-CT time under 20 minutes and a door-to-needle time under 45 minutes for eligible thrombolysis candidates.

## 1. Immediate Stabilization and Mimic Exclusion

- Point-of-care glucose must be checked immediately to exclude hypoglycemia below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L).
- Obtain a non-contrast head CT to rule out intracranial hemorrhage before preparing any fibrinolytic agent.
- Maintain systolic blood pressure below 185 mmHg before initiating thrombolytic therаpy.
- Calculate the baseline NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and confirm exact last-known-well time with family or EMS.

## 2. Thrombolysis Case Vignettes

Triage vignette: A 63-year-old woman, Clara Henderson, arrived 55 minutes after sudden right-sided hemiparesis. Non-contrast head CT showed no hemorrhage and NIHSS scored 11.

The patient weighs 70 kg with no contraindications to thrombolysis. Standard alteplase dosing at 0.9 mg/kg yields a total dose of **72 mg** (max 90 mg).

Control vignette: Patient T. (71-year-old man) presented outside the 4.5-hour window and was evaluated directly for mechanical thrombectomy after CT angiography revealed a proximal M1 occlusion.

## 3. Alteplase Dosing Breakdown

Alteplase is administered as 10% IV bolus over 1 minute followed by the remaining 90% infused over 60 minutes, capped at 90 mg total.

- For an 80 kg patient receiving alteplase 0.9 mg/kg, the total dose is 72 mg (7.2 mg IV bolus over 1 minute, then 64.8 mg over 60 minutes).
- For a 100 kg patient, 0.9 mg/kg reaches the 90 mg ceiling (9 mg IV bolus, then 81 mg over 60 minutes).
