# Small Animal Clinical Skills: Fluid Therapy Calculations

Accurate fluid resuscitation restores intravascular volume while preventing fluid overload, particularly in cats and patients with occult cardiomyopathy. Always calculate replacement deficits, ongoing losses, and daily maintenance separately before programming the infusion pump.

## 1. Dehydration Deficit and Maintenance Formulas

The dehydration replacement volume in milliliters is estimated as:

$$\text{Deficit (mL)} = \text{Body Weight (kg)} \times \%\text{Dehydration} \times 10$$

- A 10 kg dog with 6% dehydration requires a replacement deficit of 600 mL over 24 hours, which equals 25 mL/hr.
- Maintenance requirements are typically estimated at 40–60 mL/kg/day; a 4 kg cat at 50 mL/kg/day needs 200 mL/day.
- For a 20 kg dog receiving 45 mL/kg/day maintenance fluid, the daily volume is 900 mL/day (37.5 mL/hr).
- Administer maropitant 1 mg/kg SC to a 15 kg dog, giving a total dose of 15 mg once daily for acute vomiting.

## 2. Physical Exam Estimation of Dehydration

| Estimated Deficit | Mucous Membranes | Skin Turgor | Additional Clinical Findings |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 5% | Moist | Normal | Not reliably detectable on exam |
| 6%–7% | Tacky | Mild delay | Subtle corneal dryness |
| 8%–10% | Dry | Persistent tent | Mild tachycardia, sunken globes |
| > 10% | Parched | No recoil | Hypovolemic shock signs |

## 3. Renal Response and Monitoring

- Monitor azotemia daily; a drop in serum creatinine from 3.0 mg/dL (265.2 µmol/L) to 1.5 mg/dL (132.6 µmol/L) signals tubular recovery.
- Weigh hospitalized pets twice daily on the same scale; sudden weight gain above 5% suggests interstitial fluid overload.
- Auscultate lungs every 6 hours for crackles and record respiratory rate before increasing crystalloid rates.
