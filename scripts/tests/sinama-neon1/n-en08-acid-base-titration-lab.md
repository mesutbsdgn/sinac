# Analytical Chemistry: Acid–Base Titration Curves and Buffers

Danish chemist Søren Sørensen (1868–1939) introduced the pH scale at age 41 while working at the Carlsberg Laboratory. Quantitative acid–base titrimetry relies on tracking hydronium ion activity across the equivalence region to determine unknown analyte concentrations.

## 1. Strong Acid–Strong Base Titration

Consider titrating 25 mL of 0.10 mol/L HCl with standardized 0.10 mol/L NaOH in a temperature-controlled jacketed beaker.

- Before the equivalence point, excess strong acid governs pH directly from stoichiometric dilution.
- At the equivalence point of HCl and NaOH at 25.0 °C, the solution reaches pH 7.0 exactly.
- Beyond the equivalence point, excess hydroxide ions shift pH sharply above 11 within a fraction of a drop.
- Maintain magnetic stir bar clearance at 4.0 cm below the glass electrode bulb to prevent mechanical damage.

## 2. Buffer Systems and Henderson–Hasselbalch

For a weak acid $\text{HA}$ and its conjugate base $\text{A}^-$, buffer pH is approximated by:

$$\text{pH} = \text{p}K_a + \log_{10}\left(\frac{[\text{A}^-]}{[\text{HA}]}\right)$$

| Buffer System | $\text{p}K_a$ (25.0 °C) | Target pH | Stock Concentration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Acetate ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}/\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^-$) | 4.76 | 4.8 | 2.0 mol/L |
| Phosphate ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-/\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$) | 7.20 | 7.0 | 1.0 mol/L |
| Ammonium ($\text{NH}_4^+/\text{NH}_3$) | 9.25 | 9.2 | 1.0 mol/L |

## 3. Indicator Selection Principles

An acid–base indicator changes color over approximately $\text{p}K_{\text{In}} \pm 1$. Choose an indicator whose transition range lies inside the steep vertical rise of the titration curve. Phenolphthalein (pH 8.3–10.0) suits weak acid–strong base titrations, whereas methyl red (pH 4.4–6.2) matches weak base–strong acid endpoints.
