# Upper-Intermediate English Grammar: Conditional Clauses

Conditional sentences express a logical link between a condition (*if*-clause) and its consequence (main clause). Learners at B2 level typically recognize basic forms yet struggle with tense backshifting and mixed time references.

## 1. Overview of the Four Core Types

| Conditional Type | If-Clause Structure | Main Clause Structure | Primary Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Zero | Present simple | Present simple | Scientific facts and routines |
| First | Present simple | will + base verb | Likely future outcomes |
| Second | Past simple | would + base verb | Unreal present or hypothetical future |
| Third | Past perfect | would have + past participle | Regrets and counterfactual past |

## 2. Authentic Usage and Common Learner Errors

- Zero conditional for universal facts: "If water cools to 0.0 °C at standard pressure, it freezes."
- Dialogue example: "If Sarah misses the 8:00 train, she will arrive 30 minutes late to the lecture."
- Common learner mistake: "If I would have known earlier, I would call you" (correct: "If I had known earlier, I would have called you").
- Formal inversion drops *if* in academic writing: "Had the committee reviewed the draft sooner, the timeline would have stayed intact."

## 3. Mixed Conditionals

Mixed conditionals pair different time frames across the two clauses:

1. **Past condition → Present result:** "If Liam had accepted the fellowship in 2022, he would live in Edinburgh now."
2. **Present state → Past result:** "If Nora were fluent in German, she would have translated the archive herself."

Punctuation note: Place a comma after the *if*-clause when it opens the sentence, but omit the comma when the main clause comes first.

## Self-Check Questions

1. Rewrite using formal inversion without *if*: "If you should require further transcripts, please contact the registrar."
2. Explain the difference in meaning between "If she studies tonight, she will pass" and "If she studied tonight, she would pass."
3. Identify and correct the verb-tense error: "If they would have left earlier, they wouldn't have missed the ferry."
4. Construct one mixed conditional linking a past decision to a present consequence.
