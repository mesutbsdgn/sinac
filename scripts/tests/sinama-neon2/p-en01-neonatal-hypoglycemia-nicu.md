# Midwifery and NICU Nursing: Stabilization of Neonatal Hypoglycemia

**Course:** Perinatal & Neonatal Nursing Practicum — Unit 4

## 1. Risk Screening on the Delivery Unit

Infants of diabetic mothers, late-preterm newborns (34–36 weeks), and small- or large-for-gestational-age neonates require routine point-of-care glucose screening starting 30 minutes after the first feed. Early skin-to-skin contact and normothermia (axillary temperature 36.5–37.5 °C) reduce metabolic glucose consumption.

- Asymptomatic at-risk infants with borderline readings benefit from 40% oral dextrose gel massaged into the buccal mucosa alongside breastfeeding.
- Symptomatic newborns presenting with jitteriness, lethargy, apnea, or seizures require immediate intravenous access rather than oral feeding alone.

## 2. Bedside Glucose Action Thresholds

| Clinical Stage | Conventional Value | SI Equivalent | Nursing Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Critical Hypoglycemia (<4 h) | 36 mg/dL | 3.1 mmol/L | Immediate IV D10W bolus |
| Intervention Threshold (4–24 h) | 45 mg/dL | 2.5 mmol/L | Oral dextrose gel 40% |
| Target Pre-Feed Threshold (>24 h) | 54 mg/dL | 3.0 mmol/L | Continue enteral feeds |

## 3. Intravenous Dextrose Bolus and Escalation

For a 3.5 kg newborn, a 2 mL/kg bolus of D10W (100 mg/mL) delivers 7 mL (700 mg of dextrose) over 1 minute. Follow the mini-bolus promptly with a continuous D10W maintenance infusion to avoid rebound hypoglycemia.

- Recheck capillary blood glucose 30 minutes after any intravenous bolus or rate change.
- For persistent refractory hypoglycemia despite high GIR, prepare glucagon .5 mg IM/IV as a bridge while establishing central access.
- Document feeding volumes, intravenous site patency, and neurologic tone every hour during stabilization.

### Medication Order Verification

NICU order verification rule: Never write .5 mg or 5.0 mg on neonatal charts; always write 0.5 mg and 5 mg to prevent tenfold errors.
