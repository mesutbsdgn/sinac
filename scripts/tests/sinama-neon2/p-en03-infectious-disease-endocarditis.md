# Infectious Disease Pharmacy: Native Valve Infective Endocarditis

**Course:** Pharmacotherapy IV — Infectious Diseases Block

## 1. Clinical Foundations and Microbiologic Spectrum

Sir William Osler delivered his landmark Gulstonian Lectures on malignant endocarditis in 1885 at age 36, linking fever, murmur, and embolic phenomena. Native valve infective endocarditis (NVIE) requires bactericidal parenteral therapy for 4 to 6 weeks because fibrin-platelet vegetations shield bacteria from host phagocytes.

Before administering the first empirical antibiotic dose in a hemodynamically stable patient, collect three sets of blood cultures from separate venipuncture sites spaced at least 30 minutes apart.

### Clinical Pharmacokinetics Examples

Patient R. is a 48-year-old male weighing 80 kg with suspected native valve endocarditis. Empirical vancomycin is ordered at 15 mg/kg every 12 hours, yielding 1,200 mg per dose (2,400 mg/day).

For a 50 kg adult requiring a 20 mg/kg loading dose of vancomycin, the calculated initial dose is 1,000 mg IV. When enterococcal coverage requires ampicillin 200 mg/kg/day divided q6h in a 60 kg adult, each 6-hour infusion equals 3,000 mg (12 g/day).

## 3. Therapeutic Drug Monitoring and Renal Safety

Baseline renal screening confirmed serum creatinine at 1.0 mg/dL (88.4 µmol/L).

- Target vancomycin AUC/MIC between 400 and 600 mg·h/L using Bayesian software rather than relying solely on trough levels.
- Monitor serum creatinine and electrolytes twice weekly during combined vancomycin-aminoglycoside exposure.
- De-escalate vancomycin promptly to cefazolin or nafcillin if blood cultures isolate methicillin-susceptible *Staphylococcus aureus* (MSSA).
