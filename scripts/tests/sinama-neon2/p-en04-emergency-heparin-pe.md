# Emergency Medicine: Unfractionated Heparin Protocol in Acute PE

**Rotation:** Emergency Medicine Clerkship — Resuscitation Quick Guide

## 1. Risk Stratification and Anticoagulation Timing

In suspected acute pulmonary embolism (PE) with high clinical pretest probability, parenteral anticoagulation should start in the emergency department while awaiting CT pulmonary angiography unless major bleeding risk exists. Massive PE shock criteria include systolic blood pressure below 90.0 mmHg and arterial lactate above 4.0 mmol/L.

Unfractionated heparin (UFH) is preferred over low-molecular-weight heparin when thrombolysis, catheter-directed intervention, or severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) is anticipated.

An 85 kg adult arrives in the emergency department with confirmed submassive pulmonary embolism and no bleeding contraindications.
Using the standard 80 units/kg IV bolus protocol, the resident orders 5,400 units IV push followed immediately by maintenance infusion.

## 3. Peri-Intubation Adjuncts and Safety Rules

Intubating a patient with right ventricular strain carries a high risk of peri-intubation hemodynamic collapse; preload optimization and vasopressor readiness come first.

| Clinical Situation | Agent | Initial Dose | Precaution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rescue sedation (if intubated) | Midazolam IV | 2.0 mg | Titrate cautiously |
| Vasopressor support | Norepinephrine | 0.05 mcg/kg/min | Central or verified large-bore IV |

- Order-writing reminder: Never write 2.0 mg (wrong) or .5 mg (wrong) on ED medication orders; write 2 mg and 0.5 mg.
- Check anti-Xa or aPTT 6 hours after starting UFH and adjust according to the institutional nomogram.
