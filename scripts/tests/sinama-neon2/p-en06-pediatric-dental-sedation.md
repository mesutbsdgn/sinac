# Pediatric Dentistry: Local Anesthetic Ceilings and Post-Op Analgesia

**Course:** Pediatric Dental Medicine — Clinical Pharmacology Handout

## 1. Avoiding Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST)

Children have smaller body mass and lower plasma protein binding capacity than adults, making strict weight-based maximum recommended doses (MRD) essential before injecting any cartridge. A standard 1.8 mL cartridge of 2% lidocaine contains 36 mg of active drug.

Use a 27-gauge needle with 2.0 cm penetration depth for inferior alveolar nerve blocks and keep topical benzocaine contact under 1.0 minute. Always aspirate in two planes before slow deposition.

## 2. Weight-Based Local Anesthetic Ceilings

| Patient Weight | Anesthetic Solution | Maximum Limit | Calculated Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 kg child | 2% Lidocaine with epinephrine | 4.4 mg/kg max | 88 mg ceiling |
| 25 kg child | 4% Articaine with epinephrine | 7 mg/kg max | 175 mg ceiling |

- Never combine multiple local anesthetics without computing the fractional cumulative percentage of each ceiling.
- Keep lipid emulsion (20%) availability and emergency airway equipment verified in every pediatric operatory.

## 3. Postoperative Analgesia and Infection Prophylaxis

For postoperative pain in Patient C. (7 years old, 25 kg), acetaminophen 60 mg/kg/day divided q6h provides 375 mg per dose (1,500 mg/day), never exceeding the adult ceiling of maximum 4 g/day.

If dental abscess prophylaxis requires oral amoxicillin 45 mg/kg/day divided q8h for a 20 kg child, each dose is 300 mg (900 mg/day).

- Advise caregivers to use an oral dosing syringe calibrated in milliliters rather than household teaspoons.
- Warn families about soft-tissue lip biting while mandibular anesthesia remains active for 2–4 hours.
