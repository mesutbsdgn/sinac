# Environmental Chemistry: Carbonate Buffering and Ocean Acidification

**Course:** Aquatic & Environmental Chemistry — Unit 5 Study Guide

## 1. Atmospheric CO2 and Aqueous Equilibria

Swedish chemist Svante Arrhenius, at age 37 in 1896, calculated how atmospheric carbon dioxide changes influence surface temperature. When gaseous $\text{CO}_2$ dissolves in natural waters, it hydrates to carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3^*$) and dissociates stepwise into bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) and carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).

Pure water at 25.0 °C sits at neutral pH 7.0, whereas pre-industrial surface seawater averaged pH 8.2. Because pH is logarithmic, a drop of 0.1 pH units represents roughly a 26% increase in hydrogen ion activity.

## 2. Speciation Across the pH Scale

| Dominant DIC Species | Approximate pH Range | Ecological Relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dissolved $\text{CO}_2$ / $\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3^*$ | pH < 6.3 | Acidic bog waters, volcanic lakes |
| Bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) | pH 6.3 – 10.3 | Major buffer in rivers and surface oceans |
| Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) | pH > 10.3 | Required for aragonite and calcite calcification |

- Deep-sea vents can locally elevate dissolved CO2 past 1,000 ppm without altering bulk deep-ocean alkalinity.
- Rising atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ shifts equilibrium toward $\text{HCO}_3^-$, consuming $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ ions and lowering the aragonite saturation state ($\Omega_{\text{arag}}$) needed by corals and pteropods.

## 3. Total Alkalinity and Gran Titration

Total alkalinity (TA) measures the excess of proton acceptors over proton donors and remains conserved during simple $\text{CO}_2$ gas exchange, unlike pH. During Gran titration, add 5.0 mL aliquots of 0.10 M HCl until the sample drops past pH 4.5. Plotting the Gran function $F_1 = (V_0 + V)\,10^{-\text{pH}}$ against titrant volume $V$ yields a straight line whose x-intercept identifies the exact equivalence point.
