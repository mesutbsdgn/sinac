# Nursing Skills: Preparing, Reconstituting and Diluting IV Medicines

Medication errors cluster around three steps: calculating the dose, reconstituting the vial, and programming the pump. This handout walks through each step with worked numbers. Always verify against the pharmacy label and a second nurse.

## 1. The six checks before you draw anything up

- Right patient (two identifiers), right drug, right dose
- Right route, right time, right documentation
- Check the expiry date, appearance and compatibility of the diluent

## 2. Core formulas

- Volume to give (mL) = ordered dose ÷ concentration (dose per mL)
- Pump rate (mL/h) = total volume ÷ time in hours
- Gravity drip rate (gtt/min) = volume (mL) × drop factor (gtt/mL) ÷ time (min)

## 3. Worked examples

| Order | Available | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ceftriaxone 250 mg IV | 1 g vial + 10 mL diluent = 100 mg/mL | 250 ÷ 100 | 2.5 mL |
| Furosemide 30 mg IV | 20 mg/2 mL (10 mg/mL) | 30 ÷ 10 | 3 mL |
| Heparin 7,500 units SC | 5,000 units/mL | 7,500 ÷ 5,000 | 1.5 mL |
| Gentamicin 2.5 mg/kg, 18 kg child | 40 mg/mL | 45 mg ÷ 40 | 1.1 mL |
| 500 mL saline over 4 hours | pump | 500 ÷ 4 | 125 mL/h |
| 1,000 mL over 8 hours, 20 gtt/mL set | gravity | 1,000 × 20 ÷ 480 | 42 gtt/min |
| Vancomycin 1 g in 250 mL | max 10 mg/min | 1,000 mg ÷ 10 mg/min = 100 min | 150 mL/h |

## 4. Weight-based paediatric doses

Step one is always the child's measured weight, not an estimate from age.

- Gentamicin: 2.5 mg/kg/dose. For an 18 kg child that is 45 mg.
- Ampicillin 100 mg/kg/day divided q6h: for a 15 kg child that is 1,200 mg/day, or 300 mg per dose.
- Maximum single doses and daily totals must be compared with the paediatric formulary before administering.

## 5. Step-by-step: IV push of a cardiac drug

1. Verify the order and the apical pulse; hold if the pulse is below the ordered limit.
2. Prepare the syringe and label it immediately.
3. Flush the line with 5 mL of saline.
4. Give digoxin .25 mg IV push over 5 minutes while watching the monitor.
5. Flush again, document the time, dose and patient response.

## 6. Documentation and writing doses

- Never leave a naked decimal: write 0.75 mg, never .75 mg, so a missed point cannot turn it into 75 mg.
- Do not add a trailing zero: 5 mg, not 5.0 mg.
- Large numbers take a thousands separator in English-language charts: acetaminophen 1,000 mg PO every 6 hours (maximum 4 g/day).
- Spell out "units"; the abbreviation "U" is easily read as a zero.

## 7. Case: a double-check that mattered

Patient A, a 6-year-old on the paediatric ward, had an order that looked like 10 times the usual dose. The second nurse recalculated from weight (21 kg) and found a decimal-point error in the order. The pharmacist corrected it before any drug was drawn up.
