# Dental Pharmacology: Maximum Doses of Local Anesthetics

Local anesthetic toxicity in dentistry is almost always a calculation or a patient-weight problem, not a technique problem. This sheet shows the conversion chain from percent concentration to milligrams per cartridge and then to the maximum number of cartridges.

## 1. From percent to mg/mL

A 1% solution contains 10 mg per mL. So:

- Lidocaine 2% = 20 mg/mL
- Mepivacaine 3% = 30 mg/mL
- Articaine 4% = 40 mg/mL
- Prilocaine 4% = 40 mg/mL
- Bupivacaine 0.5% = 5 mg/mL

Write concentrations as "2%", not "2.0%". A bare .25 mg invites a tenfold error; always write 0.25 mg.

## 2. mg per cartridge

| Drug | Cartridge volume | mg per cartridge |
|---|---|---|
| Lidocaine 2% with epinephrine | 1.8 mL | 36 mg |
| Mepivacaine 3% plain | 1.8 mL | 54 mg |
| Articaine 4% with epinephrine | 1.7 mL | 68 mg |
| Prilocaine 4% plain | 1.8 mL | 72 mg |
| Bupivacaine 0.5% with epinephrine | 1.8 mL | 9 mg |

## 3. Maximum recommended doses

| Drug | Limit per kg | Absolute limit |
|---|---|---|
| Lidocaine (plain) | 4.4 mg/kg | 300 mg |
| Lidocaine with epinephrine | 7 mg/kg | 500 mg |
| Mepivacaine | 4.4 mg/kg | 300 mg |
| Articaine | 7 mg/kg | 500 mg |
| Prilocaine | 6 mg/kg | 400 mg |
| Bupivacaine | 2 mg/kg | 90 mg |

The lower of the per-kg value and the absolute limit applies.

## 4. Worked examples

**Adult, 60 kg, mepivacaine 3% plain.** Limit = 4.4 × 60 = 264 mg. One cartridge holds 54 mg, so 264 ÷ 54 = 4.9, and the maximum is 4 whole cartridges.

**Adult, 60 kg, articaine 4% with epinephrine.** Limit = 7 × 60 = 420 mg; 420 ÷ 68 = 6.2, so 6 cartridges.

**Adult, 70 kg, bupivacaine 0.5%.** Per-kg limit = 2 × 70 = 140 mg, which is above the 90 mg ceiling, so 90 mg applies: 90 ÷ 9 = 10 cartridges.

**Child, 18 kg, lidocaine 2% with epinephrine.** 4.4 × 18 = 79.2 mg (use the plain-solution limit for a child); 79.2 ÷ 36 = 2.2, so a maximum of 2 cartridges.

## 5. Epinephrine limits

In patients with significant cardiovascular disease the epinephrine limit is 0.04 mg per visit. A 1:100,000 solution contains 0.01 mg/mL, so a 1.8 mL cartridge delivers 0.018 mg. 0.04 ÷ 0.018 = 2.2, so no more than 2 cartridges.

## 6. Practical points

- Aspirate before every injection and inject slowly (about 1 mL per minute).
- Record the drug, concentration, number of cartridges and the patient's weight in the chart.
- Children and small adults reach the limit with far fewer cartridges than most students expect.
- Early signs of toxicity: circumoral numbness, metallic taste, tinnitus, restlessness.

## 7. Case

Patient A, a 9-year-old weighing 18 kg, needed two mandibular molars treated. The dentist planned bilateral inferior alveolar blocks and calculated that two cartridges of lidocaine would already reach the limit; the procedure was therefore split into two visits.
