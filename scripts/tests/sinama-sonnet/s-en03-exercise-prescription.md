# Physiotherapy: Writing an Exercise Prescription

A prescription is only useful if another therapist can repeat it. Describe **what, how much, how hard and how often**, then state the criteria for progressing or stopping.

## 1. The FITT framework

| Element | Question | Example (knee rehabilitation) |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | How many days per week? | 3 days/week, non-consecutive |
| Intensity | How hard? | 60–70% of 1RM, Borg CR10 of 4–5 |
| Time | How long? | 3 sets of 10 repetitions |
| Type | Which exercise? | Leg press, step-ups, bridging |

## 2. Estimating the one-repetition maximum

Testing true 1RM is unsafe in early rehabilitation. The Epley estimate uses a submaximal set:

1RM = load × (1 + repetitions ÷ 30)

- 30 kg for 8 repetitions: 1RM = 30 × (1 + 8 ÷ 30) = 30 × 1.267 = 38 kg.
- Working load at 70% of 1RM = 0.7 × 38 = 26.6 kg, rounded to the nearest available plate, 27 kg.
- 40 kg for 10 repetitions: 1RM = 40 × 1.333 = 53.3 kg.

## 3. Target heart rate (Karvonen method)

Target HR = resting HR + intensity × (maximum HR − resting HR)

For a 62-year-old client with resting HR 72:

- Maximum HR = 220 − 62 = 158 beats/min
- Heart-rate reserve = 158 − 72 = 86
- At 60%: 72 + 0.6 × 86 = 123.6, about 124 beats/min
- At 70%: 72 + 0.7 × 86 = 132.2, about 132 beats/min

## 4. Measuring progress

- Range of motion with a goniometer: record the starting angle and the end angle in degrees. Knee flexion of 120.0 degrees is a typical functional goal after total knee replacement.
- Manual muscle testing: grade 0–5. Grade 3 means the movement is completed against gravity without resistance.
- Gait speed: a speed of 1.0 m/s separates community from household walkers in many older adults.
- Strength: record dumbbell loads as numbers, for example a 5.0 kg dumbbell for the first four weeks.

## 5. Progression table

| Week | Load (leg press) | Sets × reps | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 20 kg | 2 × 12 | 90 s |
| 3–4 | 24 kg | 3 × 10 | 90 s |
| 5–6 | 27 kg | 3 × 10 | 120 s |
| 7–8 | 30 kg | 3 × 8 | 120 s |

Progress only when the client completes all sets with good form and pain at or below 3 out of 10.

## 6. Stop criteria

- Chest pain, dizziness or unusual shortness of breath
- New swelling or heat around the operated joint
- Pain that remains above baseline 24 hours after the session
- Resting blood pressure above the physician's limit

## 7. Case note

Mr. L., 62, three weeks after total knee replacement, started with the plan above. At week 2 he reported mild swelling after stairs; the therapist reduced the step height from 20 cm to a 5.0 cm platform for one week, then restarted the progression.
