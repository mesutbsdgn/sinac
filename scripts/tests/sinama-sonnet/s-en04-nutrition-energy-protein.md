# Clinical Nutrition: Energy, Protein and Feeding Rates

## 1. Resting energy expenditure (Mifflin-St Jeor)

- Men: REE = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
- Women: REE = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

| Client | Calculation | REE |
|---|---|---|
| Man, 80 kg, 180 cm, 40 y | 800 + 1,125 − 200 + 5 | 1,730 kcal/day |
| Woman, 60 kg, 165 cm, 30 y | 600 + 1,031 − 150 − 161 | 1,320 kcal/day |

Multiply by an activity factor to get total daily energy: 1.2 sedentary, 1.55 moderately active. For the man above, 1,730 × 1.55 = about 2,680 kcal/day.

## 2. Body mass index

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². For 80 kg and 1.80 m: 80 ÷ 3.24 = 24.7 kg/m². The cut-off for overweight is BMI 25.0 kg/m².

## 3. Protein targets

| Situation | Target |
|---|---|
| Healthy adult | 0.8 g/kg/day |
| Active adult, older adult | 1.0–1.2 g/kg/day |
| Critically ill patient | 1.2–2 g/kg/day |

- A 60 kg healthy adult: 0.8 × 60 = 48 g/day.
- An 80 kg active adult at 1.2 g/kg: 96 g/day.
- A 70 kg ICU patient at 1.5 g/kg: 105 g/day.

## 4. Macronutrient split

Energy values: carbohydrate 4 kcal/g, protein 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g.

For a 2,000 kcal diet with 50% carbohydrate, 20% protein and 30% fat:

- Carbohydrate: 1,000 kcal ÷ 4 = 250 g
- Protein: 400 kcal ÷ 4 = 100 g
- Fat: 600 kcal ÷ 9 = 67 g

## 5. Enteral feeding rate

A 70 kg patient needs 25 kcal/kg/day, which is 1,750 kcal. With a 1 kcal/mL formula the daily volume is 1,750 mL; over 24 hours the pump rate is 1,750 ÷ 24 = 73 mL/h.

Fluid need is about 30 mL/kg/day, so 2,100 mL for the same patient, counting the formula volume.

## 6. Refeeding risk

Severely malnourished patients who restart feeding too fast can develop hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia and heart failure.

- Start at 10–20 kcal/kg/day; a 50 kg patient begins with 500–1,000 kcal.
- Increase over 4–7 days while monitoring phosphate, potassium and magnesium.
- Start thiamine 100 mg IV and folic acid 1.0 mg daily before feeding begins.

## 7. Case examples

Case 1: Patient A, a 30-year-old athlete, needs 2,900 kcal/day to maintain weight during training; the plan adds a 300 kcal snack after each session.

Case 2: Marcus Whitfield, a 52-year-old man with cirrhosis, was referred for malnutrition. A late-evening snack with 50 g of complex carbohydrate is recommended to shorten overnight fasting.

## 8. Sodium and fluids

A low-sodium diet limits intake to 1,500 mg/day for hypertension counselling; the general public-health limit is 2,300 mg/day. Label reading and food-diary review are the two basic education tools.
