# Internal Medicine: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

DKA is the triad of hyperglycaemia, ketonaemia and metabolic acidosis. The common precipitants are missed insulin, infection and a new diagnosis of type 1 diabetes.

## 1. Diagnosis and severity

| Feature | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arterial pH | 7.25–7.30 | 7.00–7.24 | below 7.00 |
| Bicarbonate (mmol/L) | 15–18 | 10–14 | below 10 |
| Mental status | alert | alert or drowsy | stupor or coma |

Ketones are confirmed with beta-hydroxybutyrate above 3 mmol/L. A pH of 7.0 is therefore the boundary between moderate and severe disease in most guidelines.

## 2. Glucose unit conversion

Divide mg/dL by 18 to get mmol/L.

| Glucose | mmol/L |
|---|---|
| 180 mg/dL | 10 mmol/L |
| 250 mg/dL | 13.9 mmol/L |
| 360 mg/dL | 22 mmol/L |
| 540 mg/dL | 30 mmol/L |

## 3. First-hour treatment

1. **Fluids.** Isotonic saline, 15–20 mL/kg in the first hour. For a 70 kg adult that is roughly 1,050–1,400 mL.
2. **Potassium.** Check before insulin: if K+ is below 3.3 mmol/L hold insulin and replace potassium first; at 3.3–5.3 add 20–30 mEq to each litre of fluid; above 5.3, do not add and recheck every 2 hours. A serum potassium of 5.0 mmol/L is therefore already in the replace-cautiously range.
3. **Insulin.** Regular insulin 0.1 units/kg/hour by infusion; for a 70 kg adult that is 9 units/hour. The alternative is 0.14 units/kg/hour without a bolus, about 10 units/hour in the same patient.
4. **Add dextrose** when glucose falls below 250 mg/dL so ketone clearance can continue without hypoglycaemia.
5. **Bicarbonate** only if pH is below 6.9.

## 4. Laboratory arithmetic

- Anion gap = Na − (Cl + HCO3). Example: Na 132, Cl 96, HCO3 8 gives 132 − 104 = 28, a high-gap acidosis.
- Corrected sodium = measured Na + 1.6 × (glucose − 100) ÷ 100. For Na 132 and glucose 540 mg/dL: 132 + 1.6 × 4.4 = 139 mmol/L.

## 5. Resolution criteria

- Glucose below 200 mg/dL, pH above 7.3 and bicarbonate at least 18 mmol/L
- Patient eating; overlap subcutaneous insulin with the infusion for 1–2 hours before stopping it

## 6. Monitoring schedule

| Parameter | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Capillary glucose | hourly |
| Venous gas, electrolytes | every 2–4 hours |
| Urine output | hourly (aim above 0.5 mL/kg/h) |
| Mental status, vital signs | hourly |

## 7. Case: Patient A

Patient A, 24 years old and 70 kg, was brought in with vomiting and abdominal pain. Glucose 540 mg/dL, pH 7.12, K+ 4.1 mmol/L, ketones positive. After 1 L of saline the insulin infusion was started and potassium was added to the next bag. By hour 8 the anion gap had closed and the patient tolerated oral fluids.
