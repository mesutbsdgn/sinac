# Small Animal Clinical Pharmacology: Canine Dosing Worked Examples

Handout for the year-4 small animal rotation. Bring a calculator; do not rely on the practice software alone.

## Why we double-check
- Dogs range from 2 kg to over 70 kg, so one tablet size never fits all.
- A misplaced decimal on a 10 kg patient is a tenfold overdose.
- Ask a colleague to repeat the calculation without seeing your answer.

## Case 1: skin and soft-tissue infection
The dog weighs 10 kg. At 12.5 mg/kg twice daily, each dose is 150 mg.
- Drug: amoxicillin-clavulanate, oral, q12h
- Duration: 7 to 14 days, continue 48 hours past resolution

## Case 2: anaerobic enteritis
Metronidazole 15 mg/kg q12h. For the same 10 kg dog the dose is 150 mg per dose, or 300 mg/day.

| Body weight | Metronidazole 15 mg/kg q12h | Carprofen 2.2 mg/kg q12h |
|---|---|---|
| 5 kg | 75 mg | 11 mg |
| 10 kg | 150 mg | 22 mg |
| 20 kg | 300 mg | 44 mg |
| 30 kg | 450 mg | 66 mg |

Carprofen is licensed at 4.4 mg/kg/day in total, given once or split in two.

## Case 3: neuropathic pain after disc surgery
- Pain: gabapentin (10.0 mg/kg q8h) is a typical starting point, so 100 mg three times daily in a 10 kg dog.
- Reassess sedation at 48 hours; reduce the dose if the dog is ataxic.

## Case 4: the diabetic dog
A 6-year-old Labrador cross, 10 kg, newly diagnosed. Starting insulin is 0.25 units/kg q12h, which is 2.5 units per injection. Blood glucose 270 mg/dL equals 15 mmol/L (270 divided by 18).
- Creatinine 1.5 mg/dL corresponds to 132.6 µmol/L, within the range for early renal change in an older dog.

3. Why is the insulin dose given in units and not in mg?
4. Convert a glucose of 45 mg/dL to mmol/L.
