# Dental Pharmacology: Local Anaesthetic Limits

## A short history
Horace Wells, aged 29, watched a nitrous oxide demonstration in December 1844 and had his own tooth extracted without pain the next day. The use of anaesthesia in dentistry began with that tooth.

## Concentrations and cartridges
- Lidocaine 2% contains 20 mg/mL. One 1.8 mL cartridge holds 36 mg.
- Articaine 4% contains 40 mg/mL. One 1.8 mL cartridge holds 72 mg.
- Working ceiling for lidocaine 2% with epinephrine: 7 mg/kg, never more than 500 mg.

The patient weighs 70 kg. The ceiling of 7 mg/kg gives 490 mg.

| Body weight | Ceiling (7 mg/kg) | Lidocaine 2% cartridges (36 mg each) |
|---|---|---|
| 20 kg | 140 mg | 3 |
| 50 kg | 350 mg | 9 |
| 70 kg | 490 mg | 13 |

For articaine 4% in the 70 kg adult, 490 mg allows 6 cartridges (6 x 72 = 432 mg).

## Antibiotic prophylaxis
The child weighs 20 kg. Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg gives 1,000 mg, given 30 to 60 minutes before the procedure. The adult dose is 2 g.

## Writing doses safely
- Never write a bare decimal such as .5 mg; always write 0.5 mg.
- Wrong: 5.0 mg; correct: 5 mg.
- Write the unit in full after every number.

## Chairside observations

- A saline rinse at pH 7.0 is neutral and does not affect healing.
