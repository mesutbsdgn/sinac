# Physiotherapy: Loading Programme for Mid-portion Achilles Tendinopathy

Lecture 7 handout. Read before the practical session.

## Key ideas
- Tendons adapt to load; complete rest weakens them.
- Pain up to 5/10 during exercise is acceptable if it settles within 24 hours.
- Progress by load first, speed second.

## Stage 1: isometric holds
- Standing calf raise hold, 5 x 45 seconds, at about 70% of maximum effort.
- Done once daily for 1 to 2 weeks, mainly for pain relief.

## Stage 2: heavy slow resistance
- 3 sets of 15 repetitions, twice daily, which is 90 repetitions per day.
- Three seconds up, three seconds down; add a loaded backpack when 15 repetitions feel easy.

| Week | Sets x reps | Added load |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | 3 x 15 | body weight |
| 3-4 | 3 x 12 | backpack, 5.0 kg |
| 5-6 | 3 x 10 | backpack, 10 kg |

## Measuring and treating
- Measure the tender point 5.0 cm above the heel insertion to track it.
- Therapeutic ultrasound at 1.0 MHz has weak evidence for tendinopathy and is not first line.
- Topical diclofenac gel 4 g four times daily is 16 g/day; the label ceiling is 32 g/day across all joints.

## Case discussion
Patient B, a 42-year-old recreational runner, trained for a half marathon and noticed morning stiffness 3 cm above the heel. Which stage do you begin with, and what would make you refer?

## Red flags
- Sudden "pop" and loss of plantarflexion: suspect rupture, refer urgently.
- Calf swelling with warmth: exclude DVT first.

## Self-test

2. Name two criteria for progressing from stage 1 to stage 2.
