# Nursing Pharmacology: Insulin Calculations

## Historical note
Frederick Banting, aged 29, worked with Charles Best in 1921 to isolate insulin. Before then, type 1 diabetes was fatal within months.

## Units, not milligrams
Insulin is dosed in units. Write the word "units" in full and never add a trailing zero.
- Wrong: 5.0 units; correct: 5 units.
- Wrong: .5 units; correct: 0.5 units.

## Subcutaneous regimen (80 kg adult)
- Total daily dose (TDD): 0.6 units/kg/day, so 48 units/day.
- Basal is about 50% of TDD: 24 units once daily.
- The remaining 24 units are split over 3 meals: 8 units before each meal.

## Correction and carbohydrate ratios
- Insulin sensitivity factor = 1800 ÷ TDD = 1800 ÷ 48 = 37.5 mg/dL per unit.
- Carbohydrate ratio = 500 ÷ 48, about 10 g per unit.
- Glucose 270 mg/dL with a target of 120: (270 - 120) ÷ 37.5 = 4 units correction.

## Intravenous insulin in DKA
The patient weighs 80 kg. Start at 0.1 units/kg/h, which is 8 units/h. With 50 units of regular insulin in 50 mL of saline (1 unit/mL) the pump runs at 8 mL/h.

| Glucose (mg/dL) | mmol/L | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 54 | 3.0 | Treat hypoglycaemia |
| 70 | 3.9 | Lower limit of target |
| 270 | 15 | Check ketones |

## Heparin reminder
Prophylactic heparin is often 5,000 units subcutaneously every 8 hours, which is 15,000 units/day. Do not confuse the unit scale of heparin with that of insulin.
