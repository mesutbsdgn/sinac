# Emergency Medicine: Anticoagulation in Acute Coronary Syndrome

## Opening case
Maria Lindqvist, 61, arrives with crushing chest pain radiating to the left arm. ECG shows ST depression and troponin is rising. This handout works through the weight-based drugs we give in the first hour.

## First-line drugs
- Aspirin 300 mg chewed, once.
- Unfractionated heparin (UFH): 60 units/kg bolus (maximum 4,000 units), then 12 units/kg/h infusion (maximum 1,000 units/h).
- Adjust the infusion to the aPTT, not to the original weight.

## UFH dosing table

| Weight | Bolus 60 units/kg (max 4,000) | Infusion 12 units/kg/h (max 1,000) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 kg | 3,000 units | 600 units/h |
| 70 kg | 4,000 units (capped) | 940 units/h |
| 90 kg | 4,000 units (capped) | 1,000 units/h (capped) |

## Enoxaparin alternative
The patient weighs 70 kg. Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg q12h gives 70 mg per injection, 140 mg/day. Reduce to once daily if creatinine clearance is below 30 mL/min.

## Labs in the first hour
- Glucose 180 mg/dL, which is 10 mmol/L (180 ÷ 18); treat only if persistently above 180.
- Creatinine 1.1 mg/dL, which is 97.2 µmol/L (1.1 × 88.4).

## Other cases for the board
- Case B: a 55-year-old man with inferior ST elevation and hypotension; avoid nitrates.
- Mr. K. refused heparin because of a recent gastrointestinal bleed; document the shared decision.

## Pitfalls
1. Giving the full bolus without applying the cap in heavy patients.
2. Forgetting to hold the infusion before procedures.
3. Mixing the unit scales of insulin and heparin on a shared pump.
