# Music Theory II: The Dominant Seventh and Its Resolution

## Building the chord
A dominant seventh chord stacks a major triad with a minor seventh. In C major the chord on the fifth degree is G7: G, B, D, F.

- Root: G
- Major third: B
- Perfect fifth: D
- Minor seventh: F

## Why it wants to resolve
The tritоne between the third and seventh creates the tension that drives the chord toward the tonic. B wants to rise to C, and F wants to fall to E.

## Voice leading
1. Common tone: keep none; G moves to C or stays as the root of C.
2. The leading tone B rises by a half step to C.
3. The seventh F falls by a half step to E.
4. The fifth D falls by a step to C or rises to E in three voices.

## V7 in three keys

| Key | V7 chord | Tritone |
|---|---|---|
| C major | G7 (G, B, D, F) | B and F |
| G major | D7 (D, F#, A, C) | F# and C |
| F major | C7 (C, E, G, Bb) | E and Bb |

## Tuning and history
- Modern concert pitch sets A4 = 440.0 Hz, though baroque ensembles often use a lower pitch.
- Mozart, aged 8, wrote his first symphony in London, and its cadences already rely on V7 to I.

## Practice routine
- Play V7 to I in all twelve keys, hands separate, then together.
- Sing the tritone slowly and listen for the resolution.
- Write four-part voice leading for each example.
