# Introduction to Ethics: From Socrates to the Trolley Problem

## Socrates and the examined life
Socrates, aged 70, stood trial in Athens in 399 BCE on charges of impiety and corrupting the young. He left no writings; we know him mainly through Plato, who was then about 28 and in the audience.

## The Socratic method
1. Ask for a definition ("What is justice?").
2. Test it with counterexamples.
3. Expose the contradiction and refine the definition.
4. Admit what remains unknown.

## The Euthyphro question
Is an act good because the gods approve of it, or do the gods approve of it because it is good? The answer decides whether morality depends on authority or on reasons.

## Three families of theory

| Theory | Central question | Typical figure |
|---|---|---|
| Virtue ethics | What would a person of good character do? | Aristotle |
| Deontology | Which duties apply regardless of results? | Kant |
| Utilitarianism | Which act produces the most wellbeing? | Mill |

## A worked dilemma
Mr. K. finds a wallet with 500 euros and an identity card. Return it, keep it, or hand it to the police?
- Utilitarian view: compare the owner's loss with Mr. K.'s gain.
- Kantian view: could you will that everyone keep found wallets?
- Virtue view: honesty is part of a good character; so is practical wisdom about how to act.

## The trolley problem
Five people are on the track; pulling a lever diverts the trolley onto one person. Most people accept the lever but reject pushing a man off a bridge. What principle explains the difference? Case A and Case B help to separate doing from allowing.
