# Financial Accounting: Depreciation and Journal Entries

## Historical background
Luca Pacioli, aged about 47, described double-entry bookkeeping in his 1494 textbook. Every transaction still has equal debits and credits more than five centuries later.

## The asset
A delivery van costs 12,000, has a salvage value of 2,000 and a useful life of 5 years.

## Straight-line method
- Annual depreciation = (12,000 - 2,000) ÷ 5 = 2,000.
- Monthly depreciation = 2,000 ÷ 12 ≈ 166.67.
- Book value falls by the same amount every year.

## Double-declining balance
The rate is twice the straight-line rate: 2 × 20% = 40% of opening book value.

| Year | Opening book value | Depreciation | Accumulated | Closing book value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12,000 | 4,800 | 4,800 | 7,200 |
| 2 | 7,200 | 2,880 | 7,680 | 4,320 |
| 3 | 4,320 | 1,728 | 9,408 | 2,592 |

In year 4 the formula gives 1,036.8, but only 592 may be charged, so book value stops at the 2,000 salvage value.

## Journal entry for year 1 (straight-line)
- Dr. Depreciation expense 2,000
- Cr. Accumulated depreciation 2,000

## Interest on the loan
Suppose the van was bought with a loan of 20,000 at 5.0% a year. The annual interest is 1,000 (20,000 × 0.05). Ms. K., the bookkeeper, posts it as Dr. Interest expense and Cr. Cash.

## Common errors
1. Depreciating land, which has an indefinite life.
2. Letting book value fall below salvage value.
3. Posting both sides of an entry to the same account.
